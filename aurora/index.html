Restoration Challenges Aurora Homeowners Face

In Aurora, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Aurora homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Aurora began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

How Aurora Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Aurora homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your Aurora home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Water from a broken supply line or an overflowing sink is the safest kind. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. How Drying Works on an Aurora Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. On each Aurora job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. For most Aurora homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
