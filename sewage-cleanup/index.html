Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Arlington Heights, Arlington Heights Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
