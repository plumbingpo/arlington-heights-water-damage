Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Arlington Heights

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Arlington Heights crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.
